# ZK and End-to-End Verifiable Voting Architecture for a Drupal Module

**Research purpose.** This report evaluates mature end-to-end verifiable (E2E-V) voting patterns and zero-knowledge (ZK) tooling for a proposed Drupal module. It deliberately distinguishes **confirmed source facts** from **architecture conclusions**. It does not provide application code. Research was completed on 2026-09-13; dependency versions, specifications, security advisories, licenses, and audit evidence must be revalidated before any implementation or election use.

## Executive conclusion

**Conclusion.** Drupal/PHP should be an **orchestration, publication, and user-interface boundary**, not the cryptographic trust boundary. A safe first release is an explicitly low-stakes, non-governmental pilot or evidence-publication capability. It should use a separately deployed cryptographic component and publish a complete, versioned election record that independent verifiers can consume. It must not describe itself as E2E-verifiable unless it provides all three checks—**cast-as-intended, recorded-as-cast, and tallied-as-recorded**—and the corresponding evidence is independently verifiable.[11] [12]

The mature pattern is clearer than a bespoke zk-SNARK vote circuit: encrypt a well-formed ballot; publish a privacy-preserving tracker and all encrypted cast ballots to a public bulletin board; publicly prove ballot validity and tally/decryption correctness; use a challenge/spoil workflow to test the ballot-encryption device; and protect decryption with independently held threshold trustee keys. ElectionGuard documents this architecture in detail, including encrypted ballots, validity proofs, homomorphic aggregation, verifiable tally decryption, and publication of the resulting election record.[2] [3] Belenios supplies a practical reference for public ballot-box monitoring, external credential authorities, threshold trustees, and the distinction between homomorphic and mixnet tallying.[8] [9]

A PHP zk-SNARK verifier is **not recommended for V1**. The targeted Packagist search for `groth16` returned no packages, which is evidence that the default PHP/Composer ecosystem does not presently offer an identifiable candidate through that search; it does **not** prove that no PHP implementation exists anywhere.[20] More importantly, this research found no maintained, independently audited PHP verifier with a voting-appropriate assurance case to recommend. Hand-implementing pairing arithmetic, point/subgroup validation, proof parsing, and transcript binding in Drupal would substantially enlarge the trusted computing base (TCB). The right boundary is a tightly specified external verifier service—or, for client-side checks, a pinned browser verifier—while Drupal retains only validated public artifacts and verifier outcomes.

> **E2E-V is an election property, not a cryptographic-library feature.** NIST describes it in terms of observable checks on well-formedness, recording, tallying, consistent public views, and eligibility; the requirements extend beyond equipment and protocol to election procedures.[11]

This conclusion does **not** mean that ZK is unsuitable. ZK membership proofs and scoped nullifiers can be useful for anonymous eligibility and one-vote enforcement. They are an additional, difficult protocol layer, not a replacement for a verifiable encrypted tally, trustee operations, a public board, voter verification, software-distribution assurance, or coercion controls.

## Scope, vocabulary, and security claims

| Term | Meaning in this report | What it does **not** establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| **Cast-as-intended** | A voter can obtain credible evidence that the ciphertext committed by the device encodes the selections they made. The conventional mechanism is independently checkable challenge/spoil ballots. | That a cast ballot was opened, or that a compromised voter device did not display one choice while encrypting another without an effective audit workflow. |
| **Recorded-as-cast** | The voter finds the tracker for the exact accepted encrypted ballot in the published record. | That the ballot was valid, that every eligible voter participated, or that the tracker is non-coercive. |
| **Tallied-as-recorded** | Any observer verifies the published tally derives from the published accepted ballots using the specified cryptographic proofs. | Eligibility, resistance to malware, availability, or ballot secrecy if keys/endpoints are compromised. |
| **Public bulletin board (PBB)** | A publicly readable, canonical, append-only evidence log containing election configuration, ballot artifacts, status transitions, proofs, tally artifacts, and signed checkpoints. | Immutability merely because it is a database or “blockchain.” Equivocation remains possible unless independent monitors compare views and checkpoints. |
| **Tracker / receipt** | A non-secret identifier or hash commitment allowing the voter to locate an accepted encrypted ballot. | A proof of how a voter voted. A receipt that demonstrates vote choice enables vote buying/coercion. |
| **Nullifier** | A deterministic, scoped public value accepted at most once; in a ZK membership design it enforces one signal/vote per eligible secret and scope. Semaphore explains the identity nullifier, external nullifier, and nullifier-hash relationship.[19] | Anonymous eligibility if issuance logs, scope reuse, metadata, or a weak/known credential can identify the voter. |
| **Mixnet** | A sequence of verifiable re-encryption/permutation steps, followed by decryption of shuffled ballots. | Privacy if every mix server colludes or input/output side channels remain linkable. |
| **Homomorphic tally** | Addition of encrypted selections to decrypt only aggregate contest totals. ElectionGuard uses this with proofs that ballot selections and contest limits are valid.[2] | Support for arbitrary ranked, write-in, or complex ballot contents without a different construction. |
| **Threshold decryption** | A quorum of trustee/guardian key shares is required to decrypt the final aggregate. ElectionGuard describes distributed shares that let a quorum recover if some guardians are missing.[3] | Endpoint secrecy, correct trustee behavior, or availability if fewer than the threshold participate. |

The following claims are deliberately bounded. Cryptographic proofs can make a false published tally detectable **assuming** the protocol, parameters, implementations, ceremony, public record, and verification process are correct. They do not prevent a compromised device, a camera, a coerced voter, a stolen credential, denial of service, registration fraud, or an operator from suppressing voter participation. ElectionGuard’s 2024 analysis likewise notes that guardian checks do not prevent a ballot-marking device from leaking nonces or using a broken random generator, and it requires guardians to refuse decryption requests for individual cast ballots.[1] NIST separately notes that E2E verification does not itself cover registration fraud or ballot stuffing unless relevant procedural/evidence checks are also performed.[11]

## Established approaches and what to reuse

### ElectionGuard: strongest architectural reference for a constrained tally

**Confirmed facts.** ElectionGuard specifies three user-facing E2E-V capabilities: a verification code, a mechanism to challenge/spoil a ballot, and publication of encrypted artifacts and zero-knowledge proofs for independent verification.[2] Its published process uses guardians, an election public key, encrypted zero/one selections, non-interactive validity proofs, homomorphic aggregation, and verifiable decryption.[3] The public election record is intended to contain the manifest/context, submitted encrypted ballots, spoiled-ballot decryptions, encrypted tally, decrypted tally, guardian data, and related constants—never guardian private keys.[5] The current official specification landing page lists version 2.1 and reports changes such as guardian-record verification, ballot identifiers, revised confirmation-code handling, and updated decryption protocols.[4]

**Architecture conclusion.** For simple plurality, approval, and bounded-selection contests, model the protocol and record format on ElectionGuard rather than inventing a Circom circuit to prove ballot well-formedness. Use an independently reviewed ElectionGuard-compatible cryptographic engine or a purpose-built service with a frozen, published protocol profile. A Drupal module can own election workflow, content presentation, authorization to start/close an election, storage of *public* evidence, status display, and links to independent verification. It must not hold guardian private keys, derive randomness, fabricate proofs, or be the sole verifier.

### Helios: valuable open-audit precedent, but an explicit low-coercion warning

**Confirmed facts.** The original Helios paper presents browser voting, encrypted ballots, audit/cast separation, public auditability, and an explicit design target of elections where coercion is not a serious concern.[6] The current Helios FAQ says that ballot encryption occurs in the browser, only an aggregate is decrypted, and multiple trustees may be designated; it also states that online elections should not be used where significant fraud or coercion is expected.[7]

**Architecture conclusion.** Helios is an appropriate reference for browser-side encryption and tracking, not an argument that a remote Drupal voting form is suitable for public-office or high-stakes elections. Its cast-or-audit idea is required inspiration for a claimed cast-as-intended property. A tracker alone proves only inclusion of an artifact, not correct encryption of the voter’s intent.

### Belenios: practical separation of roles and tally-mode selection

**Confirmed facts.** Belenios states that its public ballot box lets voters see accepted ballots and that anyone can verify the tally; its ballots are signed by voter credentials.[8] Its operational instructions separate voter, administrator, credential authority, trustee, committee, and auditor duties. The instructions require auditors to monitor configuration consistency, ballot-box evolution, ballot proofs, delivered client code, and decryption proofs.[9] Belenios supports a homomorphic mode for simple selections and an experimental mixnet mode for rankings/grades; it documents threshold decryption and external credential authority as security-improving operational choices.[8] Its 2024 release notes also show why “mature” must not be confused with “finished”: it fixed a reported distributed-key-generation issue with an additional signature check.[8]

**Architecture conclusion.** Reuse Belenios’ separation-of-duties model even if its code is not reused. In particular, never combine Drupal administrator, credential issuer, trustee, verifier publisher, and monitor roles in a single account/service. For a capable existing online-election platform, a lower-risk option is to integrate Drupal only as an election catalogue/SSO-facing portal and refer voters to a separately operated voting system, rather than reimplement its protocol inside Drupal.

### Mixnets versus homomorphic tallying

| Ballot/tally need | Preferred mature construction | Evidence to publish | Privacy and operational trade-off | V1 decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Binary selections, approval, bounded “choose up to *k*” contests | Additively homomorphic encryption with zero/one/range and contest-limit proofs; decrypt aggregate totals only. | Manifest, encrypted cast ballots, ballot-validity proofs, aggregate ciphertexts, trustee decryptions/proofs, signed board checkpoints. | Does not expose individual votes during tally. It is substantially simpler than decrypting all ballots. | **Use if E2E-V pilot proceeds.** Restrict ballot types to this class. |
| Rankings, grades, arbitrary write-ins, complex ballots | Verifiable re-encryption mixnet then decrypt shuffled valid ballots; apply public count rule. | Input set, each mix output, shuffle proofs, trustee decryption proofs, final clear shuffled ballots and count transcript. | Better suited to non-additive ballots, but larger operational and verification surface. Timing/format/metadata can still link inputs. | **Defer.** Do not add under a “simple” Drupal release. |
| Anonymous eligibility with one-vote enforcement | ZK membership proof against a fixed credential commitment root plus election-scoped nullifier and ballot commitment binding. | Group-root history, verification key/circuit hash, public inputs, proof-acceptance decision, nullifier set, ballot commitment. | Adds ceremony, circuit, verifier, issuance, revocation, replay, and correlation risks. | **Experimental later phase only.** |

The architecture conclusion is not that one method is universally more private. Homomorphic tallying minimizes individual ballot decryption for additive contests. Mixnets accommodate richer ballots but require credible shuffle proofs and careful traffic/metadata controls. Belenios’ documented deployment distinction supports this functional trade-off.[8] [9]

## ZK tooling assessment

### Circom and circomlib

**Confirmed facts.** Circom is a Rust compiler and domain-specific language for arithmetic circuits. It produces an R1CS constraint system plus C++ or WebAssembly witness-generation programs, supports parameterized templates, and points users to static-analysis tools for weak/strong circuit safety.[13] Circomlib describes itself as a library of circuit templates/cryptographic primitives, not as a voting protocol.[14]

**Conclusion.** Circom is suitable for a **future, carefully isolated eligibility circuit**—for example, proving membership in a committed eligible-voter set, correct nullifier derivation, and binding an encrypted-ballot commitment to the proof’s public inputs. It is **not** sufficient by itself for voting. Reusing a Poseidon, Merkle-path, comparator, or signature template does not demonstrate that the composed circuit has no under-constraint, incorrect range bound, domain-separation error, or public-input mismatch. Every circuit change alters the security-critical statement and must trigger test vectors, review, and—where a real election claim is made—independent cryptographic audit. Pin exact compiler, circuit source, dependencies, R1CS, WASM, proving key, verification key, hash function, field/curve, and test vectors. Treat those hashes as election parameters before voting opens.

### snarkJS, Groth16, PLONK, and FFLONK

**Confirmed facts.** snarkJS is a JavaScript/Pure WebAssembly toolkit that works in Node.js and browsers and supports Groth16, PLONK, and FFLONK.[15] Its documentation says Groth16 requires a circuit-specific ceremony; a zero-contribution `zkey` is unsafe for production; the tool verifies contribution transcripts and exports a verification key.[15] It describes PLONK and FFLONK as not needing a circuit-specific ceremony beyond a universal powers-of-tau ceremony, but labels FFLONK **beta**.[15] The Groth16 paper presents a three-group-element proof and three pairings for verification.[16] The PLONK paper describes an updatable universal structured reference string (SRS), while the FFLONK paper reports a verifier-efficiency/prover-time trade-off.[17] [18]

| Item | Confirmed capability | Relevant risk or limitation | Recommendation for this module |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Circom** | R1CS circuit compiler with WASM/C++ witness calculation and composable templates.[13] | Circuit relation is security-critical; compiler/template reuse does not prove the relation matches election rules. | Use only after an explicit circuit-security program; not necessary for homomorphic ballot validity proofs. |
| **circomlib** | Reusable basic circuit templates/primitives.[14] | Generic library, no election protocol assurance stated. | Candidate source of primitives only; pin revision and independently review the instantiated circuit. |
| **snarkJS** | Browser/Node proof generation and verification for Groth16, PLONK, FFLONK.[15] | JavaScript/WASM supply chain, memory/CPU denial of service, artifact/version mismatch; FFLONK is beta in its own docs. | Use as a pinned **client utility or isolated verifier implementation**, never as Drupal/PHP cryptography. Do not select FFLONK for V1. |
| **Groth16** | Very compact proof/verification construction.[16] | Circuit-specific trusted setup; toxic-waste failure/ceremony governance is a protocol risk. | Only if compact proof size is decisive and a transparent multi-party ceremony plus reproducible transcript verification is funded. Otherwise defer. |
| **PLONK** | Universal/updatable SRS design and snarkJS support.[15] [17] | “Universal” reduces per-circuit ceremony work; it does not remove the need to trust/check a properly generated SRS, audit the circuit, or bind artifacts. | Better default than Groth16 for a future Circom proof pilot, subject to external review and concrete benchmark/compatibility tests. |
| **FFLONK** | A PLONK variant aimed at lower verifier group-operation cost.[18] | snarkJS labels its FFLONK support beta.[15] The cited paper is a preprint. | Exclude from V1 and from the first security-critical pilot. |
| **Semaphore V4** | Maintained generic anonymous-interaction stack with circuits, JS libraries, and off-chain/on-chain verification support.[19] Its V4 release changed the Merkle-tree and identity schemes and is not backward-compatible with V3.[19] | Generic group-signaling protocol, not a complete election protocol; group/issuance authority, scope design, membership updates, and voter-device risks remain. | Possible later dependency for an anonymous eligibility proof; use current V4 artifacts only after protocol review. Never reuse V3 assumptions/keys/circuit IDs. |

### PHP verifier decision

**Finding.** No maintained/audited PHP zk-SNARK verifier was identified that can responsibly be recommended for a Drupal voting module. This is an **absence-of-qualified-evidence conclusion**, not a universal impossibility claim. The main Composer registry returned no results for `groth16` in the direct search performed for this report.[20] By contrast, the Rust Arkworks Groth16 repository explicitly labels itself an academic proof-of-concept and “NOT ready for production use,” demonstrating why a language change alone is not assurance.[25]

**Decision.** Do not implement or embed a PHP pairing-based Groth16/PLONK verifier. PHP should parse only a schema-constrained **verification attestation/result** after an external verifier has independently parsed and checked the actual proof and public inputs. The verifier result alone is not an election artifact: store and publish the proof, public inputs, verification-key digest, circuit/protocol identifier, verifier build digest, and canonical artifact hashes so other verifiers can reproduce the decision.

## Recommended architecture and trust boundaries

### Component model

| Component | Responsibilities | May hold | Must not hold or decide |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Drupal module** | Election workflow; role separation; manifest publication; presentation; evidence-index API; status transitions; publication of signed board checkpoints; link to independent verifier. | Public manifest and contest hashes; public ballots/proofs; public tracker; public tally record; service credentials with narrow scope. | Voter secret/credential preimages, witness data, proving keys, guardian shares, plaintext cast ballots, verifier private signing keys, or sole authority for verification. |
| **Eligibility/credential authority** | Establish eligibility through an election-specific process; issue anonymous credential commitments/tokens if used; publish public credential root and signed issuance parameters. | Mapping from voter identity to issuance event only under strict policy; private issuance secrets. | Cast ballot contents, tally decryption shares, public board mutation authority beyond signed root updates. |
| **Browser voting client** | Render a pinned manifest; construct encrypted ballot; locally verify pre-submission artifacts; generate optional membership proof; offer cast or spoil before final cast; show tracker. | Selections and witness temporarily, using audited client code and CSPRNG. | Long-term private keys/credential backups by default; a coercer-verifiable plaintext receipt. Client success is never server acceptance. |
| **Cryptographic verifier service** | Strictly parse fixed protocol/circuit artifacts; verify ballot proof and optional membership ZK proof; enforce public-input binding; emit a signed, reproducible decision. | Public verification keys, circuit/parameter allowlist, service signing key/HSM reference, limited request metadata. | Guardian shares, plaintext selections, eligibility identity mapping, arbitrary circuits/keys submitted by a client. |
| **Public bulletin board / evidence store** | Immutable-versioned publication of signed events and complete downloadable election record; Merkle roots/checkpoints; public query by tracker. | Public artifacts only. | Voter identity mapping, guardian shares, credential preimages, private audit logs. |
| **Trustees/guardians** | Independently create/share election decryption material; verify pre-election record and aggregate to be decrypted; create threshold partial decryptions/proofs. | Each trustee’s protected key share. | Any private cast-ballot decryption request, credential issuance mapping, or unilateral tally decision. |
| **Independent monitor/verifier** | Fetch records from multiple paths; check checkpoints/equivocation; independently verify accepted-ballot and final-tally evidence. | Public artifacts. | Dependence on Drupal session or administrator authorization to audit. |

This separation follows ElectionGuard’s insistence that guardians check the record and do not decrypt individual cast ballots,[1] Belenios’ operational separation of credential authority, trustees, and auditor,[9] and NIST’s requirement that public checks not be limited to privileged actors.[11]

### External and local proof-verifier design

A **local verifier** is useful for voter experience and independent auditing. Ship it as a reproducible, content-addressed static bundle or downloadable tool with a pinned release/signature and published test vectors. It can verify a proof before submission and verify the final election record without trusting Drupal. It **cannot** be the admission authority: a malicious client could skip it or submit a different artifact.

An **external verifier service** is the admission authority for cryptographic acceptance. Deploy it on a separate host/account/network segment from Drupal. It must accept only a small, versioned request schema; exact manifest hash; fixed election UUID; proof-system ID; allowed verification-key hash; expected ordered public inputs; proof bytes with strict size/encoding limits; and the ciphertext/ballot-commitment hash that the proof binds. It must reject unknown fields, duplicate fields, non-canonical encodings, unrecognized curves, malformed points, wrong subgroup/field representations as applicable to the selected library, expired election state, repeated nullifier, mismatched circuit/verification key, or proof replay across elections. The verifier must bind the **election context hash**, contest/manifest hash, ballot ciphertext commitment, and nullifier scope—not merely a generic “membership is valid” statement.

Drupal may accept a ballot only after it receives a verifier decision signed over the canonical request hash, verification-key/circuit identifiers, service build digest, and decision. It must atomically persist the accepted artifact and the unique `(election_context, nullifier)` or one-time authorization token. A later verifier result cannot mutate the accepted ballot. The raw proof and public inputs remain in the public record. Do not call a command-line prover/verifier through PHP, pass arbitrary paths, accept arbitrary `zkey` files, or allow an admin UI to alter a cryptographic artifact after the election is opened.

Use mutually authenticated TLS or an equivalent strongly authenticated internal channel, endpoint-specific authorization, strict content-type/schema validation, request-size/time/CPU ceilings, audit logs that exclude secrets, and separate administrative endpoints. OWASP recommends HTTPS for REST services, endpoint-level access control, and considers mutually authenticated client certificates for highly privileged services.[21] These are necessary service controls, not a substitute for protocol security. The verifier should have no outbound network access while verifying and should run under a dedicated identity with bounded CPU, memory, queue, and rate limits to prevent proof-based denial of service.

### Public bulletin-board and election-record design

A relational Drupal database is a **cache/index**, not the authoritative evidence format. Define a canonical, versioned export with deterministic serialization and an accompanying schema. ElectionGuard provides a useful precedent: its serialization specification exists specifically to enable compatible implementations and independent verifier authors.[4] Each board event should have a domain-separated hash of canonical content, a previous-event hash, an event type, election context, timestamp from an auditable policy, server signature, and periodic signed Merkle-root checkpoint. Publish checkpoints through at least one channel operationally independent of the primary Drupal host. Monitors should obtain and compare complete record snapshots repeatedly; a hash chain does not detect a split view if all monitors see the same operator-controlled endpoint.

The pre-election package should freeze: election UUID; human-readable manifest; canonical manifest hash; contest rules and selection limits; public election key; guardian public records; quorum; proof-system/circuit/verification-key parameters if ZK eligibility is enabled; credential-root policy; nullifier derivation/scope; client/verifier artifact hashes; opening/closing rules; and board checkpoint public keys. Any material alteration creates a **new election context**, not an in-place edit.

At close, publish all accepted encrypted ballots and their validity proofs, rejected-artifact reason categories without identity data, nullifier set if used, aggregate ciphertexts, trustee partial decryptions/proofs or the protocol’s combined evidence, final tally, result signature, complete manifest, source hashes, and board roots. Never include private keys, witness data, encryption nonces for cast ballots, voter identity/credential mappings, IP addresses, session IDs, raw user-agent data, or plaintext cast ballots. ElectionGuard’s record guidance similarly excludes guardian private keys and enumerates the public tally artifacts.[5]

## Privacy, linkability, receipt, and coercion failure modes

| Failure mode | Why it matters | Required mitigation/boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity-to-ballot join in Drupal** | If the authenticated Drupal UID, login event, session, email, or authorization record sits with the submitted ciphertext/tracker, operators can reconstruct who cast which encrypted ballot. Future cryptanalytic breaks or coercion may turn that into vote disclosure. | Split eligibility issuance from ballot acceptance; persist only anonymous one-time authorization/nullifier at the ballot boundary. Keep any legally necessary identity logs in a different system with separate access/policy. Do not put user IDs in board data or tracker URLs. |
| **Scoped-nullifier reuse** | The same nullifier scope across elections or poll questions lets observers link a participant’s actions, even if they cannot identify the person. | Domain-separate scope with protocol version, election UUID, immutable manifest hash, and explicit purpose; never reuse an election scope. Bind proof signal to the ballot commitment to stop proof transplant. |
| **Issuer correlation / small anonymity set** | The credential issuer can correlate issuance time, network metadata, or a unique group update with a later proof/nullifier. A small contest may reveal vote patterns after tally. | Separate issuers/ballot box; batch issuance; minimize and segregate logs; offer a privacy relay only after threat-modeling its risks; avoid publishing fine-grained timestamps/device metadata; state that anonymity is limited in small elections. |
| **Tracker as vote receipt** | A tracker that includes plaintext choice, encryption nonce, deterministic vote encoding, or a coercer-checkable opening enables vote buying/coercion. | Publish an opaque tracker/ciphertext hash only. Keep cast-ballot nonce secret. Permit opening only of spoiled/challenged ballots. Document that a remote client cannot guarantee receipt-freeness against screen capture or coercer-controlled devices. |
| **Challenge workflow leaks a cast vote** | Reusing a challenged ciphertext, nonce, or tracker as the cast ballot turns audit information into a proof of vote. | Each challenge is irrevocably marked spoiled, opened publicly, excluded from tally, and followed by a freshly encrypted cast ballot. The system must enforce this state transition in the board. |
| **Replay / duplicate vote** | A valid membership proof can be replayed, or a repeated authorization token can cause double counting. | Make nullifier/authorization uniqueness an atomic admission rule; bind to election context and ballot commitment; reject duplicates; publish the accepted nullifier set. Semaphore specifically notes that anonymous proofs must be prevented from being verified twice to avoid repeated voting.[19] |
| **Ballot stuffing or silent deletion** | Public proof verification cannot show that only legitimate voters cast or that missing voters checked trackers. | Publish signed eligibility-root/count commitments; separate credential authority; monitor board changes; provide complaint/escalation process; reconcile turnout and issued credentials under an approved policy. NIST notes this gap explicitly.[11] |
| **Client malware / biased randomness** | A malicious device can mis-encrypt, leak nonces, expose credentials, or communicate selections. | Use challenge/spoil, code integrity monitoring, reproducible clients, independent verification on another device where feasible, and a low-stakes boundary. Do not claim endpoint privacy from ZK alone. |
| **Remote coercion / credential transfer** | A coercer can watch a voter, direct selections, demand credentials, or prevent voting. | No claim of coercion resistance for V1 remote voting; avoid high-stakes elections. Helios makes the same low-coercion deployment boundary.[6] [7] |
| **Timing, transport, and observability metadata** | IPs, access logs, CDN/WAF records, TLS fingerprints, device type, or submission order can link identity to a board entry. | Data-minimization/retention policy; segregated logs; avoid third-party analytics on election paths; operational review of CDN/WAF access logs; batch public publication when compatible with recorded-as-cast requirements. |
| **Threshold-key collusion or loss** | A quorum may decrypt, while too few available trustees prevent completion. | Choose threshold through documented risk/availability analysis; independent trustees; hardware-protected keys where justified; backup/recovery ceremony; signed key-generation record; no individual-ballot decryptions. Key material needs lifecycle, compromise, and protection procedures consistent with NIST key-management guidance.[22] |

Privacy protection must be asserted only under stated assumptions. For example, threshold encryption can prevent a subset below quorum from decrypting the aggregate, but does not stop a voter endpoint from leaking its choice. A ZK membership proof can hide the membership witness from the verifier, but it does not erase web-server or issuer metadata. A public tracker supports recorded-as-cast, but it can pressure a voter to demonstrate participation. These tensions require a written threat model and user-facing disclosure before any pilot.

## Safe V1 boundaries

**V1 should be a constrained, non-binding or organizational low-coercion pilot.** It may support a fixed simple ballot model only: one or more binary/approval contests with explicit selection limits; fixed, pre-published manifests; no free-text write-ins; no ranked/graded/STV ballots; no vote weights; no revoting; and no anonymous eligibility ZK unless separately completed as an audited experimental feature. Use an externally operated cryptographic engine and publish deterministic sample records before accepting real ballots.

V1 must not be marketed as suitable for public-office, governmental, high-stakes, union, or coercion-sensitive elections. The National Academies concludes that current Internet-voting risks outweigh benefits and recommends pilots of E2E-V systems using paper ballots, with risk-limiting audits for public-election outcomes.[23] The EAC’s VVSG is the current U.S. voting-system standards framework, but following this report does not constitute VVSG conformance, certification, legal compliance, accessibility conformance, or a security audit.[24]

The minimum V1 deliverable is a **verifiable evidence pipeline**, not a “ZK voting feature” checkbox. It must freeze pre-election artifacts, accept only externally verified artifacts, produce an exportable record, support tracker lookup without login, and provide an independent offline verifier path plus monitoring instructions. If it lacks cast/spoil and independently verifiable ballot construction, call the result **recorded-and-tallied evidence publication**, not E2E-V. If it stores an identity-to-ballot mapping, explicitly call it **authenticated secret-ballot workflow with operator-linkability**, not anonymous voting.

No PHP proof verifier, private-key handling, custom pairing arithmetic, on-chain dependency, blockchain token, write-ins, mixnet, automatic credential issuance, or “coercion-resistant” claim belongs in V1. The board should avoid a blockchain by default: a signed transparency log with independently run monitors is sufficient to test the core evidence design, while a ledger does not solve endpoint coercion, identity correlation, or implementation correctness.

## Concrete recommendations

1. **Adopt an ElectionGuard-style public evidence model now, not a bespoke SNARK ballot circuit.** Keep the first ballot type additive and bounded. Copy the *architecture*, record completeness, challenge/spoil workflow, and independent-verifier expectation; do not casually claim compatibility with a specification unless every relevant serialization/protocol requirement is implemented and tested.[2] [3] [4]

2. **Make Drupal a non-cryptographic coordinator.** Put encryption, proof verification, tallying, and trustee operations in separately deployed, minimally privileged components. Use public hashes and fixed schemas at every interface. Do not permit Drupal administrators to change a manifest, verification key, circuit, tally mode, or trustee quorum after opening.

3. **Reject PHP zk-SNARK verification for V1.** The evidence review did not identify a maintained/audited PHP candidate. Do not build one. Use a pinned browser verifier for local checks and a separately deployed verifier service for admission; require independent offline verification of the published record.

4. **If a future ZK eligibility feature is necessary, choose PLONK over Groth16 as the initial evaluation baseline, not a production default.** The evidence supports PLONK’s universal/updatable SRS model and snarkJS support.[15] [17] It does not eliminate need for a ceremony/SRS integrity process, circuit audit, artifact pinning, or secure service boundary. Exclude FFLONK while its snarkJS support is beta.[15]

5. **Use a strict public-input binding contract.** The proof must bind to the election UUID/context hash, frozen manifest hash, eligible-root version, nullifier scope, nullifier, and exact encrypted-ballot commitment. Verify these exact values before board admission. Proof validity without this binding is not vote validity.

6. **Design the PBB as an exportable transparency log.** Publish signed hash-chain/Merkle checkpoints and complete canonical record bundles. Recruit at least one monitor independently operated from the Drupal host. Store only public artifacts on the board; aggressively exclude direct identifiers and linkable operational metadata.

7. **Separate credential issuer, ballot box, trustees, administrators, and auditors.** At minimum, use different principals and access domains. Belenios’ deployment guidance shows that combining credential authority and decryption roles materially weakens the security story.[9] A threshold protects against less-than-quorum key compromise, but only with a real trustee ceremony and operations plan.

8. **State remote-voting limits prominently.** V1 must warn about malware, credential theft, denial of service, participation coercion, and the fact that receipt-freeness/coercion resistance is not claimed. Restrict use to approved low-coercion contexts. Treat a paper-backed, risk-limited-audit path as a prerequisite for any public-election discussion.[23]

9. **Require independent verification as a release condition.** Before real ballots, an external party must reproduce a complete test-election tally from the published record using a verifier not supplied by the election operator. If only Drupal’s own UI can say “verified,” the central E2E-V value proposition is unmet.

## Verification acceptance checklist

Before accepting any pilot ballot, the election operator should be able to demonstrate the following statements with public artifacts rather than administrator assurances:

- The pre-election manifest, cryptographic parameters, trustee public information, proof/circuit/verification-key identifiers, and client/verifier hashes were published and then frozen.
- A voter can independently spoil/challenge a ballot and see the disclosed challenged ballot match their selections; a challenged ballot is publicly excluded from the tally.
- The accepted cast-ballot tracker locates exactly one public encrypted ballot and does not reveal a vote opening or voter identity.
- Every accepted ballot is accompanied by a verified well-formedness proof and, where used, a membership proof whose public inputs bind to the correct election and ballot commitment.
- Nullifier or one-time authorization uniqueness is enforced atomically, and the accepted public set is available for independent duplicate detection.
- The final aggregate is recomputed from accepted ballots, and threshold decryption evidence verifies against the frozen election public key.
- At least one independent tool, operated outside the Drupal/election-operator environment, reproduces the final acceptance set and tally from the downloadable record.
- The published board record contains no private keys, witness data, cast-ballot nonce/opening, voter identity mapping, or unnecessary metadata; log-retention and incident-response procedures are documented.

Failure of any item should block a claim that the election is E2E-verifiable. Passing the items remains a bounded integrity claim and does not by itself establish coercion resistance, endpoint security, legal validity, accessibility, availability, or suitability for a public election.
